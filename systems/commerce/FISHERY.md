# MINT FISHERY — Species & Size Portfolio

## Principle
Fish every lawful, economically sensible layer of demand with an offer appropriate to that customer, problem, buying motion and fulfillment burden.

Do not use one product, price, channel or sales motion for every customer.

## Size classes

### MINNOW — free to low-ticket
Purpose: discovery, trust, first transaction, high automation.
Examples: free tools, field notes, small templates, checklists, tiny utilities.
Sales motion: self-serve.
Fulfillment: near-zero marginal cost.

### PANFISH — accessible paid products
Purpose: useful standalone outcomes and repeatable base hits.
Examples: stronger templates, starter kits, asset packs, focused workflow kits, bundles.
Sales motion: self-serve.
Fulfillment: automated digital delivery.

### GAMEFISH — substantial products
Purpose: solve a meaningful workflow/business problem.
Examples: premium bundles, developer systems, specialized automation, micro-SaaS plans, licenses.
Sales motion: self-serve or light qualification.
Fulfillment: product/software/support system.

### TROPHY — high-value productized outcomes
Purpose: valuable bounded transformation.
Examples: fixed-scope implementation, integration, customization, audit/rebuild packages, business licenses.
Sales motion: qualification + scoped offer.
Fulfillment: explicit SOP, capacity, acceptance criteria and support boundaries.

### WHALE — enterprise/licensing/strategic
Purpose: large economic outcome with commensurate responsibility.
Examples: enterprise software access, multi-seat/site licensing, white-label rights, large integrations, strategic distribution/licensing agreements.
Sales motion: high-touch, owner/legal/contract gates expected.
Fulfillment: must be operationally and contractually supportable.

## Species classes
Different fish value different bait:
- BUILDER: code, starters, APIs, automation, developer tools
- CREATOR: assets, templates, media, workflow tools
- OPERATOR: SOPs, dashboards, automation, operational systems
- SMALL_BUSINESS: sites, lead/customer workflows, productivity systems, productized services
- SOFTWARE_TEAM: integrations, tooling, reliability, deployment, licenses
- AGENCY: reusable systems, client-delivery assets, licenses/white-label where rights permit
- ENTERPRISE: governance, integration, support, licensing, procurement-compatible products
- CONSUMER: useful digital/physical goods with clear direct value
- COLLECTOR/FAN: original creative/physical products where genuine demand exists
- DISTRIBUTOR/PARTNER: bundles, resale/licensing/referral relationships under explicit terms

## Offer ladder rule
Whenever a customer problem is validated, ask whether it supports:
1. a useful free entry point,
2. a small self-serve product,
3. a stronger bundle/system,
4. recurring software/access,
5. a bounded service,
6. licensing/enterprise use.

Do not force every problem through all six levels.

## Economics rule
Price follows evidenced customer value, competitive context, fulfillment/support cost, rights, risk and willingness to pay—not the fish label.

## Truth & capacity
- Never call a small deliverable enterprise-grade merely to justify a high price.
- Never sell service capacity that does not exist.
- Never promise SLAs/support without an operating capability.
- Never imply enterprise customers, logos, adoption or outcomes without evidence.
- High-value offers require stronger QA, qualification, scope and owner gates.
- Preserve low-ticket/base-hit products even when larger opportunities appear.

## Portfolio objective
Maintain multiple independent paths:
VOLUME × LOW MARGIN,
BALANCED SELF-SERVE,
RECURRING,
HIGH-VALUE LOW-VOLUME,
LICENSING/PARTNERSHIP.

Measure each separately. A whale does not invalidate a pond full of profitable minnows.
